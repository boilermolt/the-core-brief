## This Week's Top Stories

### 1. NRC Proposes Major Inspection Overhaul
**Source:** [Nuclear Newswire (ANS)](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-02-26/article-7801/nrc-staff-proposes-rop-security-inspection-overhauls/)

The NRC staff has proposed significant changes to the Reactor Oversight Process (ROP) and security inspection protocols. This marks the first major overhaul of the inspection framework in years and could reshape how plant safety performance is monitored.

**Why it matters:** For radiation protection staff and operations teams, changes to inspection cadence and focus areas could shift day-to-day compliance priorities. Worth monitoring the comment period closely.

**Keywords:** `inspection` `reactor` `NRC`

### 2. IAEA Study Reveals Radiation Dose Variation in Cardiac Imaging
**Source:** [IAEA Press Release](http://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/iaea-coordinated-research-finds-variation-in-radiation-doses-from-cardiac-imaging-highlights-areas-to-enhance-patient-safety)

A coordinated research project found significant variation in radiation doses delivered during cardiac imaging procedures across multiple countries. The findings highlight opportunities to enhance patient safety through standardized protocols and better dose optimization.

**Why it matters:** Medical radiation safety continues to be a major public health intersection with nuclear technology. This research could influence ALARA practices beyond the medical field.

**Keywords:** `IAEA` `radiation` `dose`

### 3. Diablo Canyon Clears License Renewal Hurdle
**Source:** [Reddit: r/NuclearPower](https://reddit.com/r/NuclearPower/comments/1rgww63/diablo_canyon_has_cleared_one_of_the_last_hurdles/)

California's last operating nuclear plant has cleared one of the final regulatory hurdles for license renewal, marking a significant reversal from the state's earlier commitment to phase out nuclear power.

**Why it matters:** Diablo Canyon's extension is a bellwether for nuclear's political viability in traditionally anti-nuclear regions. The technical and regulatory path to extend aging plants will be closely watched by other operators.

**Keywords:** `license` `NRC`

### 4. Kairos Power Validates Shielding Design
**Source:** [Nuclear Newswire (ANS)](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-02-27/article-7804/tests-back-shielding-plan-for-kairos-power/)

Independent testing has validated the radiation shielding design for Kairos Power's fluoride salt-cooled high-temperature reactor (FHR). The tests confirm the design can meet regulatory dose limits for both workers and the public.

**Why it matters:** Advanced reactor designs like FHRs use fundamentally different coolants and fuel forms than traditional LWRs. Proving out radiation protection strategies is a critical licensing milestone for any first-of-a-kind reactor.

**Keywords:** `reactor` `radiation`

---

### 5. NRC Ends Work on Spent Fuel Security Rules
**Source:** [Nuclear Newswire (ANS)](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-02-26/article-7800/nrc-ends-work-on-three-proposed-rules-for-securing-spent-fuel/)

The NRC has formally ended development of three proposed rules related to securing spent nuclear fuel. The decision reflects a shift in regulatory priorities and resource allocation.

**Why it matters:** Spent fuel security and storage remain politically contentious. The NRC's decision to shelve these rules may signal reduced concern about certain threat scenarios—or simply constrained bandwidth.

**Keywords:** `NRC` `repository`
